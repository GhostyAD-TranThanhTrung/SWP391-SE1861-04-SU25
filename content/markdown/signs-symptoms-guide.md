# Signs and Symptoms Guide

## Recognizing Mental Health Warning Signs

Early identification of mental health concerns is crucial for effective intervention and treatment. This guide helps you recognize common signs and symptoms that may indicate the need for professional support.

## General Warning Signs

### Emotional Changes
- Persistent sadness or hopelessness
- Excessive worry or anxiety
- Extreme mood swings
- Irritability or anger outbursts
- Feeling emotionally numb
- Loss of interest in activities once enjoyed

### Behavioral Changes
- Withdrawal from friends and family
- Significant changes in eating or sleeping patterns
- Decline in personal hygiene
- Increased use of alcohol or drugs
- Risk-taking behaviors
- Self-harm or talk of suicide

### Physical Symptoms
- Unexplained aches and pains
- Chronic fatigue
- Frequent headaches
- Digestive problems
- Changes in appetite or weight
- Sleep disturbances

### Cognitive Changes
- Difficulty concentrating
- Memory problems
- Indecisiveness
- Confusion or disorientation
- Racing thoughts
- Difficulty completing tasks

## Age-Specific Signs

### Children (Ages 5-11)
- Frequent nightmares or sleep problems
- Excessive clinginess or separation anxiety
- Regression in behaviors (bedwetting, thumb sucking)
- Frequent temper tantrums
- Difficulty in school
- Social withdrawal

### Adolescents (Ages 12-18)
- Dramatic changes in personality
- Difficulty with relationships
- Academic decline
- Risky sexual behavior
- Substance experimentation
- Self-injury behaviors

### Adults
- Inability to cope with daily activities
- Relationship problems
- Work performance issues
- Financial difficulties due to poor judgment
- Neglecting responsibilities
- Social isolation

### Older Adults
- Confusion or memory loss beyond normal aging
- Increased dependence on others
- Neglecting personal care
- Increased physical complaints
- Social withdrawal
- Loss of interest in previously enjoyed activities

## Specific Mental Health Conditions

### Depression Signs
- Persistent sad, empty, or hopeless feelings
- Loss of interest in activities
- Significant weight loss or gain
- Sleep disturbances
- Fatigue or loss of energy
- Feelings of worthlessness or guilt
- Difficulty concentrating
- Thoughts of death or suicide

### Anxiety Disorders
- Excessive worry about everyday situations
- Restlessness or feeling on edge
- Easily fatigued
- Difficulty concentrating
- Irritability
- Muscle tension
- Sleep problems
- Panic attacks

### Bipolar Disorder
**Manic Episodes:**
- Elevated or irritable mood
- Increased energy or activity
- Decreased need for sleep
- Racing thoughts
- Distractibility
- Poor judgment
- Risky behavior

**Depressive Episodes:**
- Same symptoms as major depression

### Eating Disorders
- Extreme concern with body weight or shape
- Severe restriction of food intake
- Binge eating episodes
- Excessive exercise
- Use of laxatives or diet pills
- Social withdrawal around meals
- Preoccupation with food

### Substance Use Disorders
- Inability to control use
- Continued use despite negative consequences
- Tolerance (needing more to achieve same effect)
- Withdrawal symptoms when not using
- Neglecting responsibilities
- Giving up important activities
- Continued use despite health problems

## Crisis Warning Signs - Seek Immediate Help

### Suicide Risk
- Talking about wanting to die or kill oneself
- Looking for ways to kill oneself
- Talking about feeling hopeless or having no purpose
- Talking about feeling trapped or in unbearable pain
- Talking about being a burden to others
- Increasing use of alcohol or drugs
- Acting anxious, agitated, or reckless
- Sleeping too little or too much
- Withdrawing or feeling isolated
- Showing rage or talking about seeking revenge
- Displaying extreme mood swings

### Psychosis Warning Signs
- Hallucinations (seeing or hearing things that aren't there)
- Delusions (false beliefs)
- Disorganized thinking or speech
- Bizarre or inappropriate behavior
- Severe social withdrawal
- Decline in personal hygiene
- Loss of touch with reality

## When to Seek Help

### Immediate Help Needed
- Thoughts of suicide or self-harm
- Thoughts of harming others
- Severe confusion or disorientation
- Hallucinations or delusions
- Substance overdose
- Severe panic attacks

### Professional Help Recommended
- Symptoms persist for more than two weeks
- Symptoms interfere with daily functioning
- Multiple warning signs are present
- Family or friends express concern
- Previous mental health conditions
- Significant life stressors

## How to Help Someone

### What to Do
- Express concern and care
- Listen without judgment
- Encourage professional help
- Offer to help find resources
- Stay connected and supportive
- Take suicide threats seriously

### What Not to Do
- Don't minimize their feelings
- Don't offer simple solutions
- Don't promise to keep secrets about self-harm
- Don't leave them alone if at immediate risk
- Don't try to diagnose or treat them yourself

## Resources for Help

### Emergency Situations
- Call 911 or go to nearest emergency room
- National Suicide Prevention Lifeline: 988
- Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741

### Non-Emergency Support
- Contact primary care physician
- Reach out to mental health professionals
- Contact employee assistance programs
- Use online mental health screening tools
- Connect with support groups

## Remember

- Mental health conditions are treatable
- Early intervention leads to better outcomes
- Seeking help is a sign of strength, not weakness
- Recovery is possible with proper support
- You don't have to face this alone

If you or someone you know is experiencing these warning signs, don't wait. Reach out for help today. Mental health is just as important as physical health, and professional support can make a significant difference in recovery and overall well-being.
